Breach a challenge and the firm immediately offers you a discounted reset — a tempting, cheap-feeling way to get right back in. Sometimes it's the smart move. Just as often it's the start of throwing good money after bad. The price question is easy; the discipline question is the one that actually matters.

## The price math (the easy part)

A reset restarts your existing challenge at a discount; a new account is a full-price fresh purchase. So on cost alone:

| Option | Typical cost | Use when |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Reset | Discounted | You want the same account/plan again |
| New account | Full price | You want a different size, plan or drawdown type |

If you're retrying the same setup, the reset wins on price. If you want to *change* the parameters — a different [drawdown type](/guides/prop-firm-drawdown-explained/), a smaller size, a different plan — a new account may be worth the extra. That's the whole price story.

## The discipline question (the part that matters)

Before you spend anything, diagnose *why* you breached:

- **Fixable one-off** — you misread a rule, made a single sizing error, had one revenge-trading lapse you now understand. A reset is reasonable; you've earned a retry.
- **Systemic** — loose [risk management](/guides/prop-firm-risk-management/), a strategy that isn't ready, or a recurring emotional pattern. A reset here just funds the same loss again, at a discount that feels like a bargain and isn't.

The firm offers the reset the instant you breach precisely because that's the moment you're most emotional and least likely to diagnose honestly. Sit with the cause before you click.

## Weight retries into your true cost

Every reset is part of the real price of getting funded. A "cheap" challenge that takes four resets to pass wasn't cheap. This is exactly what the [true challenge cost calculator](/tools/challenge-cost-calculator/) captures — fee divided by a realistic pass rate — and why a firm with [forgiving drawdown](/guides/prop-firm-drawdown-explained/) and a higher effective pass rate can be cheaper overall than a discount firm you keep resetting. If your total spend across attempts is climbing without progress, the [fee recovery calculator](/tools/fee-recovery-calculator/) will show how much you now need just to break even — usually the clearest signal to pause and fix the cause rather than buy another retry.

The honest rule: **fix first, pay second.** A reset after a real fix is an investment. A reset instead of a fix is just a slower way to lose.
