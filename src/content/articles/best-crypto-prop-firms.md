Crypto breaks the assumptions prop-firm rules were built on. Those rules were designed around forex — a market with sessions, a daily close, and volatility measured in fractions of a percent on the majors. Crypto has none of that: it trades every hour of every day and can move 5% while you sleep. Choosing a crypto prop firm is mostly about finding one whose rulebook survives that reality.

## Start with the universal non-negotiables

No amount of crypto-specific polish rescues a firm that doesn't pay. Before anything crypto-specific, apply the same filter as [any firm ranking](/best/best-prop-firms/): documented payout reliability, fair [drawdown structure](/guides/prop-firm-drawdown-explained/), real cost to funded, and firm stability. The crypto segment skews young, and youth is exactly the risk factor behind most [firm collapses](/guides/are-prop-firms-legit/). An established multi-asset firm offering solid crypto CFDs usually beats a brand-new crypto-only firm on every metric that gets you paid.

## Then check the four crypto-specific things

Once a firm clears the reliability bar, weigh what makes crypto different:

| Check | Why it matters for crypto |
|-------|---------------------------|
| Weekend / 24-7 trading | Crypto never closes — a firm that treats weekends as shut caps a real part of your edge |
| Drawdown width | A % limit fine for forex is easily breached by one normal crypto swing |
| Coin coverage | BTC/ETH are universal; the altcoins you trade may not be offered |
| Overnight / funding fees | Holding CFD positions across days on a 24/7 asset accrues costs quickly |

**Weekend trading and reset timing** deserve special attention. On a market with no session close, "daily" drawdown reset times can behave in ways designed for forex — know exactly when your counter resets, or a weekend position can breach you at an hour you didn't expect. It's the same discipline as knowing your reset time in [drawdown rules](/guides/prop-firm-drawdown-explained/), just with higher stakes.

## Size for volatility, not for comfort

The single biggest crypto mistake is carrying forex position sizing into a market that moves several times faster. A stop that's "wide" on EUR/USD is tight on BTC, and a position that respects your daily limit on a calm major can blow through it on a normal crypto candle. Run every crypto trade through the [position size calculator](/tools/position-size-calculator/) using crypto's real range, then confirm the worst case sits inside your [breach levels](/tools/drawdown-calculator/). On crypto, undersizing isn't caution — it's survival.

## The practical shortlist approach

Rather than chase a "crypto-only" label, build your shortlist like this:

1. Take the [most reliable firms](/best/best-prop-firms/) — most crypto CFDs sit at [forex firms](/best/best-forex-prop-firms/) — and check which offer the coins you trade.
2. Filter for **weekend trading** and the **coins you actually trade**.
3. Among those, prefer the **widest static drawdown** for volatility headroom.
4. Weight the fee by a realistic pass rate with the [true cost calculator](/tools/challenge-cost-calculator/) — crypto's swings can lower effective pass rates, so the cheapest sticker price is rarely the cheapest path to funded.

Do that and you end up with a firm that treats crypto as a first-class market and still pays like a serious one — which is the whole point.
