You can pass a challenge on luck. You cannot *stay funded* on it. The traders who turn a funded account into income all share one trait, and it isn't a secret entry signal — it's risk management disciplined enough to survive variance long enough for a real edge to pay. Here's the playbook.

## The four rules that keep accounts alive

1. **Risk small and fixed.** A consistent 0.5–1% per trade lets a positive edge compound while keeping any single trade far from a breach. Some 2026 firms now [cap this explicitly](/guides/prop-firm-drawdown-explained/) — a sign of where the industry is heading.
2. **Never widen risk to chase.** Increasing size to hit a target or recover a loss is the single most common way accounts die. The size that was right at the start of the day is right at the end of it.
3. **Set a personal daily stop.** Stop at 40–50% of the firm's daily limit (−2% against a 5% rule). It makes an official breach nearly impossible and interrupts revenge-trading before it compounds.
4. **Size for the streak, not the trade.** Your worst *realistic* losing run must stay inside your drawdown — the whole point of [how to pass a challenge](/guides/how-to-pass-a-prop-firm-challenge/) done right.

## Survival is the strategy

Here's the counterintuitive part: a modest edge sized conservatively beats a strong edge sized recklessly, because the reckless account gets breached before its edge can express itself. This is what a [risk of ruin calculation](/tools/risk-of-ruin-calculator/) makes visible — two traders with identical edges have completely different outcomes purely from position size. If your risk of ruin is in double digits, the answer is never a better strategy; it's smaller risk per trade.

## Risk management is also a growth strategy

The same discipline that keeps you funded is what lets you *scale*. Firms reward low-risk consistency with larger accounts and higher splits, so the trader who manages risk well doesn't just survive — they compound, as covered in [scaling plans explained](/guides/prop-firm-scaling-plans-explained/). Reckless sizing forfeits both the account and the growth. Protect the downside relentlessly, and the upside takes care of itself.
